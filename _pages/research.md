## Job Market Paper

**Small Bites, Big Effects? Snacking and Health in the U.S.**
_Job Market Paper_

**Abstract**
American children’s diets fall well short of federal dietary guidelines, and snacking now accounts for about a quarter of their daily calories. This paper studies whether higher habitual snacking causally affects diet quality and body composition. Using NHANES data from 2003 to 2018, I implement a bunching design at zero snack consumption to estimate the marginal effect of additional snack calories on the Healthy Eating Index (HEI) and BMI z-scores. I find that an extra 100 kcal of daily snacking reduces the HEI total score by 0.52 points. The effect declines with age and is broadly similar across income groups. In contrast, I find no detectable effect on BMI z-scores and can reject economically large weight effects. The results suggest that policy should focus less on eliminating snacks and more on improving the nutritional quality of what children snack on.
