## Teaching Statement

My teaching goal is to help students develop both strong economic intuition and practical analytical skills. I design classes that connect core theory to policy-relevant questions and real-world evidence, so that students can move confidently from concepts to application. In practice, this means combining clear exposition of economic mechanisms with empirical examples, guided problem-solving, and active discussion. I want students to leave my courses not only understanding models, but also knowing how to interpret data, evaluate identification strategies, and communicate economic reasoning clearly.

I aim to create an inclusive and supportive classroom environment where students with different backgrounds can engage with rigorous material. To do this, I use structured lectures, frequent low-stakes practice, and transparent grading expectations, while encouraging participation through discussion prompts and short applied activities. In quantitative courses, I emphasize step-by-step intuition before technical formalization; in field courses, I focus on linking empirical findings to institutional context and policy design. Across settings, I treat teaching as an iterative process and continuously improve course delivery based on student feedback and learning outcomes.

My teaching experience spans undergraduate and graduate courses in microeconomics, econometrics, labor, urban, and health economics. These experiences have strengthened my commitment to rigorous, student-centered instruction and prepared me to teach a wide range of economics courses at the university level.
